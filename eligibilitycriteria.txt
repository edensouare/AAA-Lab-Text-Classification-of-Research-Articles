Exclusion Criteria:

1. Studies measuring anatomical and structural, electrophysiological, histological and/or outcomes and where genomic, proteomic or metabolomic outcomes are measured in addition to anatomical, histological or neurochemical outcomes.

2. Studies focused on agriculture, energy, resources, materials science, drug discovery, or other industrial/technical development

3. Neurotechnology or biomedical research restricted to laboratory or pre-clinical contexts

4. AI studies centered on algorithm development, performance optimization, or hardware implementation without a health/mental health focus

5. AI applications in domains like logistics, resource management, or machine efficiency without clear relevance to health or mental health

6. Not published in the English language.

7. Non-academic research paper

Inclusion Criteria: 

1. Any article providing primary data of an dependency of AI overreliance or AI addiction

2. Studies on problematic dependence, addiction, or excessive immersion leading to psychological or social dysfunction

3. Research addressing AI use and dependence

4. Analyses of mechanisms such as cognitive dependence (delegating information processing or judgment to AI), relational dependence (preferring AI interaction over human interaction), loss of autonomy, reduced self-efficacy, or social isolation

5. Research comparing variations across demographic factors (e.g., age, gender, socioeconomic status, digital literacy)

6. AI Overreliance

Studies examining excessive or uncritical reliance on artificial intelligence, algorithms, or automated systems.(examples include: Automation bias – the tendency to accept AI outputs even when erroneous. Over trust in AI – lack of critical evaluation of AI recommendations. Decision-support dependency – situations where humans defer judgment entirely to AI systems.)

7. AI Addiction

Studies addressing compulsive or addictive patterns of AI use. (Examples include: Excessive engagement with conversational AI systems (e.g., ChatGPT, Replika). Reward-driven or compulsive use of AI-powered gaming or social media platforms. Loss of control or self-regulation failure in AI use.)

8. AI Dependency

Studies focusing on reliance on AI for everyday tasks or cognitive functions. (Examples include: Cognitive offloading – delegating memory, problem-solving, or decision-making to AI. Learned helplessness – inability to function effectively without AI assistance. Skill degradation – diminishing human expertise or performance due to persistent AI reliance.)
